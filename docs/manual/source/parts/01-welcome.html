DM LockGuard is a website and phone app that lets a building's owner decide who can open which door, when, and from which phone, and it writes down every single attempt.

Think of it as a very organised, very honest security guard who never sleeps. The guard holds the master list of who's allowed in, checks every request, passes approved ones to the lock, and keeps a diary of everything that happened.

What LockGuard does

{i:lock} Locks & unlocks

Open or lock your doors from a phone or computer. Works with the Eufy Smart Lock C30 (model T85D0).

{i:users} Manages people

Seven ready-made roles, from the all-powerful Super Administrator to a cleaner who only gets Tuesdays.

{i:smartphone} Ties people to phones

Each person registers one phone. A stolen password alone can't open your door.

{i:mail-plus} Invites & trains

Send an email invitation; Pip the paperclip teaches the newcomer before their phone works.

{i:siren} Watches for trouble

Repeated wrong passwords, jammed locks, low batteries and lost phones raise alerts.

{i:scroll-text} Keeps a diary

A tamper-evident audit log records who did what, when, and from where.

How it works: the big picture

When you tap Unlock on your phone, the request takes a short trip. Your phone never talks to the lock directly, and it never sees the Eufy password. Everything goes through LockGuard, which checks you first.

The always-on computer that runs LockGuard. Your phone asks it questions; it answers. The "brain" in the back room. The app you see is the front desk; the backend is where decisions are made. The API is the hatch they pass notes through. A translator. LockGuard speaks "LockGuard", Eufy speaks "Eufy". The bridge speaks both. Someone else's computer, far away. The Eufy cloud relays messages to your lock.

The one promise: LockGuard never fibs about your door

A lot of apps show "Unlocked!" the moment you tap the button. LockGuard doesn't. It shows Unlocking… while the command travels, and switches to {i:lock-open} Unlocked only after the lock itself reports it. If the lock goes quiet, LockGuard says {i:shield-alert} State unknown rather than guessing.

"Unknown" means the lock hasn't confirmed anything recently. It might be locked, it might not. Never assume. Go and look, or ask someone nearby. Then try again. I asked the lock if it was open. It said, "I'd rather not disclose that until I've confirmed it." Very secure. Very annoying at parties.

What LockGuard does not do (yet)

  • It doesn't work with other lock brands. Today it supports the Eufy Smart Lock C30 (T85D0).
  • It doesn't manage the keypad codes stored inside the lock. It keeps records about codes, but changing the real code is done in the Eufy app.
  • Proximity ("walk up and it opens") unlocking isn't available yet, and no role includes it.
  • Building-wide "lockdown" holds are not implemented yet.

Pick the path that matches you.

If someone invited you

  1. Open the email called "You're invited to DM LockGuard" and tap Accept Invitation. Use the phone you'll open doors with.
  2. Create your account. Your email is already filled in. Choose a password of at least 12 characters with letters and numbers.
  3. Do the training with Pip. Seven short lessons. Each one has a "Your turn" task; the Next button wakes up when you finish it.
  4. Pass the quiz. Five questions, and you need all five right. Miss one? Pip gives a hint and you try again. No limit, no shame.
  5. Register your phone. Enter your password once more. Done. Your phone is now your key.
If you were given a registration code instead of an email (it looks like PZZE-R4CQ), sign in on your phone and type it when asked. It works once and expires.

If you're setting LockGuard up

  1. Install it on a Windows or Linux server: see Chapter 3. Allow an afternoon the first time.
  2. Create the first Super Administrator with php artisan lockguard:create-admin.
  3. Connect your Eufy account through the bridge and register your locks.
  4. Set up email under Settings → Email settings so invitations can be delivered.
  5. Invite people under Access management → Invitations, then give each person access to the right locks on their user page.
Setting up a security system is like making a sandwich. Skip a layer and things get messy, and somebody else ends up eating it.
