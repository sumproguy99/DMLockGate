LockGuard has two faces. On a computer you get the full control centre with a sidebar. On a phone you get a simple app with big buttons and a bar along the bottom. Same LockGuard, same rules, same account.

Signing in

If you're bound to one phone and sign in on a different device, LockGuard asks for a registration code instead of letting you in. That's device binding doing its job (see Chapter 8).

The phone layout

Open LockGuard on a phone and it switches to the mobile app automatically (the address ends in /m). The bottom bar has four buttons:

HomeYour doors, with big Lock/Unlock buttons.
ActivityWho opened what, and when (if you may view history).
DevicesYour doors' details and your registered phone.
MoreAccount, location sharing, notifications, help, sign out.
On iPhone, tap Share → Add to Home Screen; on Android, tap ⋮ → Add to Home screen. LockGuard then opens like a normal app, full-screen, with its own icon.

Anatomy of a lock card

  1. Name and place: "Front Door · E2E Home · T85D0" (door, property, lock model). Tap Details for more.
  2. The big status: what the lock itself last reported, and how long ago.
  3. Health badges: {i:wifi} Connected and {i:battery-medium} Battery 80%, plus when the lock last checked in.
  4. Buttons: Lock and Unlock. You only see the ones your role allows.
  5. The receipt: after a command, a checklist shows each stage: Request received → Authorization completed → Command submitted → Integration response received → Physical state confirmed.
