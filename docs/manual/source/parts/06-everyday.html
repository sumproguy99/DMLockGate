Sign in (and sign out)

  1. Open LockGuard at your building's address (for example https://lockguard.example.com).
  2. Enter your email and password and tap Sign in.
  3. If asked, type the 6-digit code from your authenticator app.
  4. To sign out: on a phone, More → Sign out; on a computer, click your name (top right) → Sign out.
After too many wrong attempts LockGuard makes you wait a few minutes (too_many_requests) and the Security Administrator gets an alert. That's to stop password-guessing robots, not to punish you.

Turn on two-step sign-in

Two-step sign-in means a thief needs your password and your phone. It takes two minutes.

  1. Install an authenticator app on your phone: Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, 1Password, Authy… any will do.
  2. In LockGuard, open the Account page (phone: More → Account) and choose Two-step verification.
  3. Tap Open in authenticator app on the phone, or type the key shown into the app.
  4. Type the 6-digit code the app shows, and confirm your password.
  5. Save your recovery codes somewhere safe (a password manager or printed in a drawer). Each one works once if you lose your phone. They're shown only once.
A second lock on your account. Even if someone learns your password, they still need the code that only your phone can make, and it changes every 30 seconds. Some roles must use two-step sign-in (administrators, to start with). If yours does, LockGuard asks you to set it up straight after you sign in, and you can't remove it. Got a new phone? Choose Move to a new phone on the Account page, then set up the new phone straight away. Don't screenshot them into your photo album, and don't email them to yourself. If you use one, the Security Administrator is told, in case it wasn't you.

Change your password

  1. Open the Account page (on a phone More → Account) and find Change password.
  2. Type your current password, then the new one twice: at least 12 characters, with letters and numbers.
  3. Tap Change password. You stay signed in here; every other browser and app is signed out, and you get a "password was changed" email.
If an administrator created your account and told you a password, LockGuard asks you to choose your own the first time you sign in, before anything else (even two-step sign-in). Type the password you were given as the current one.

Forgot your password?

  1. On the sign-in page, tap "Forgot your password?" and type the email of your LockGuard account.
  2. Open the email "Reset your DM LockGuard password" and tap Choose a new password. The link works once, for an hour. Check spam if it isn't there after a few minutes.
  3. Pick a new password: at least 12 characters, with letters and numbers, and not the one you had.
  4. Sign in again on your registered phone. A reset signs you out everywhere, and you get a "password was changed" email.
The page always says a link is on its way, even for an address LockGuard doesn't know, so nobody can use it to find out who has an account. A reset never skips the other locks: you still need your registered phone and, if you use it, your authenticator code. Suspended accounts, accounts still doing their invitation training, and the kiosk account can't reset this way: ask your administrator. Got a "password was changed" email you didn't expect? Tell your administrator straight away.

"Confirm it's you" (step-up)

Before risky actions (unlocking remotely, sending an invitation, changing settings) LockGuard may ask for your password or code again, even though you're already signed in. That proof lasts about 10 minutes.

Like a cashier asking for ID only when you buy something expensive. You're already in the shop; this is just for the big stuff.

Your phone and other devices

Open Devices to see every phone and browser signed in to your account.

{i:shield-check} Trust this device

Marks a device as one you keep with you. Your administrator can require a trusted device for unlocking. Only trust your own phone, never a shared or work computer.

{i:log-out} Sign out everywhere else

Ends every other session at once, while keeping this one signed in. Use it if you signed in on a friend's laptop and forgot to sign out.

{i:pencil} Rename

Give devices names you'll recognise: "Riley's iPhone" beats "Safari on iOS 19".

{i:smartphone} Registered phone

With device binding on, one phone is the phone for your account. A new phone needs a new registration code.

Got a new phone?

  1. Ask your administrator for a registration code. They get one from your user page (Devices), or at the server with lockguard:device-code.
  2. On the new phone, open LockGuard, sign in, and type the code when asked (it looks like ABCD-1234).
  3. Done. Ask for the old phone to be revoked if you're giving it away.

Lost your phone? Do this right away

  1. From any computer, sign in and use Sign out everywhere else, or call your Property Manager or Security Administrator.
  2. Ask them to Report lost / Revoke device on that phone. It stops working immediately, and any attempt to use it raises an alert.
  3. Change your password with Forgot your password? on the sign-in page if the phone wasn't locked with a PIN.
  4. Get a registration code for your new phone.
  5. If the lost phone had your authenticator app and you don't have your recovery codes, ask a Super Administrator or Security Administrator to reset your two-step sign-in, then set it up on the new phone.

Location sharing

Some setups use your phone's location for access decisions or safety. Here's exactly what happens:

{i:map-pin} What is recorded

Where your phone is when you press Lock or Unlock in the app, and, if an administrator asks, your current location while LockGuard is open on your phone. Nothing is tracked in the background.

{i:eye} Who can see it

Administrators with the location-history permission (by default only the Super Administrator). Every look is itself recorded, and Auditors can check who looked.

{i:history} How long it's kept

30 days by default, then deleted automatically every night.

{i:list} Your own history

More → Location and proximity shows each time your location was shared. You can always see your own record.

Your browser asks permission the first time. Location only works on secure (https://) addresses; that's a browser rule, not a LockGuard one. An invisible circle drawn on the map around the building. Inside the circle: "near the door". Outside: "away".

Notifications

The {i:bell} bell shows a number when something needs your attention: an alert, an invitation update, a lock that went offline. Open Notifications to read them and mark them read. Security Administrators always receive security notifications. LockGuard caps notifications at 20 per hour per person, so a broken lock can't bury you.

Choose how you're told. Open Notifications > Preferences (on a phone: Notifications > Notification preferences). For each kind of alert (security, removed devices, lock problems, batteries, the lock integration) tick In LockGuard, By email, both or neither, then Save preferences. Emails go to your account's address; ask an administrator to change it.

Your administrator sets the limits: whether alerts are emailed at all, how serious an alert must be before it's emailed (Medium and above to start with), and which kinds you can't turn off in the app (security alerts and removed devices to start with). If you handle security incidents, you always see security alerts in LockGuard and always get High and Critical ones by email. A failed email is tried three times; administrators see each delivery under Settings > Notification settings.

Change your name, email or password

For safety, people can't change these themselves. Ask your Property Manager or Super Administrator. (This stops someone who borrows your unlocked phone from taking over your account.)

A good password is long rather than complicated. Four random words, like correct-horse-battery-staple, is easier to remember and harder to crack than P@ssw0rd!.

An invitation is the friendliest way to add someone. They get an email, create their own account, learn how LockGuard works with Pip the paperclip, pass a short quiz, and only then can their phone open a door.

For the person sending it

  1. Access management → Invitations → Invite someone. Enter email, name, roles (if you may assign roles) and an optional personal note.
  2. Send and confirm your password. LockGuard emails a link that works for 7 days, and holds a registration code for that email address.
  3. Track progress in the list. Resend makes a fresh link (the old one stops working) and gives 7 more days. Cancel kills the link and the held code.
  4. After they finish, give them lock access on their user page. Invitations carry roles, not door access.
If email isn't set up yet, the invitation is still created, but LockGuard shows a warning with a link to Email settings. Fix the settings, then press Resend. Also ask the person to check their spam folder.

For the person receiving it

Meet Pip, a cartoon paperclip in a security guard's cap. Pip waves, points, thinks, cheers and occasionally looks very surprised. Pip will:

  • walk you through creating your account
  • teach 7 short lessons, one small part at a time
  • give you a "Your turn" task in every part: tap, sort, put in order, or try the practice door
  • share a Food for thought idea and a joke after each task
  • give you a 5-question quiz (all answers must be right; hints if not)
  • register your phone at the end
Stopped halfway? Just open the invitation email and tap Accept Invitation again. You'll continue where you left off.
