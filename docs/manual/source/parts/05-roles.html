Every LockGuard account has a role. A role is a ready-made bundle of permissions: small "yes, you may" switches such as Lock a door or Suspend an account.

Instead of handing out keys one at a time, you hand out key rings. "Resident" is a key ring; "Super Administrator" is the janitor's giant key ring. One single "you're allowed to do this" switch. If the switch isn't on, the answer is no.

{i:shield-check} The server decides

Hiding a button isn't security. LockGuard's server checks every permission on every request, so even a clever person with a hacked app gets "no".

{i:lock} Doors are given separately

A role says what you may do; lock access says which doors. Even a Super Administrator can't open a door until they've been given access to it.

{i:user-cog} Overrides exist

An administrator can add a single extra permission (or block one) for one person, without inventing a new role.

The comparison chart

This is the whole story on one page. Read a column to see everything one role can do, or a row to see who can do one thing. ✓ = included, ✗ = not included, ! = not by default (see the note below). Click a role's name to jump to its section.

Every unlock sent through the LockGuard app counts as a remote unlock, so it needs two permissions: lock.unlock and lock.remote_unlock. Out of the box only the Super Administrator and Resident roles have both. Property Managers, Temporary Guests and Service Providers can lock from the app but won't see an Unlock button until an administrator adds the "Request remote unlocking" permission to that person as an individual override (here's how).

How many permissions each role has

There are 35 permissions in total. Bigger bar = bigger key ring.

Nobody gets "Walk-up proximity unlock" from a role, not even the Super Administrator. It's deliberately left out and has to be granted on purpose, one person at a time, once the feature exists.

How the roles relate to each other

Think of three layers. The Super Administrator runs the whole system. Two managers look after it: the Property Manager handles people and access, and the Security Administrator handles threats and safety. Below them, four door users and an observer use or review it.

The three golden rules between roles

{i:ban} No self-service

Nobody can change their own account, roles or door access from the website. Ask another administrator. (Error self_modification_forbidden.)

{i:arrow-up} No promoting past yourself

You can only hand out administrative powers you already have. A Property Manager can't create a Security Administrator. (Error target_outranks_actor.)

{i:key-round} Door access is the exception

Everyday door permissions (lock, unlock, history, location) aren't "admin powers", so a manager may give them even though they're not part of their own job.

Sneakily giving yourself (or a friend) a bigger key ring. LockGuard simply won't let you.

Who is tied to one phone?

With device binding on (the default), everyone signs in only on their one registered phone, except the Super Administrator and Security Administrator, who may need to respond from any computer during an emergency.

Scenario
D

Dana owns a 12-flat building

Super Administrator

Dana installs LockGuard over a weekend. On Saturday she registers the Front Door and Bike Store locks, turns commands on, and sets up Gmail in Email settings. On Sunday she invites Marcus as Property Manager and Priya, an IT friend, as Security Administrator. From Monday on, Dana mostly glances at the Dashboard: Marcus handles the tenants, and Priya gets the alerts. Dana still has the master key ring; she just doesn't need to carry it around every day.

How to: add a property and register a lock

  1. Properties → Add a property. Give it a name (e.g. "Maple Court") and its time zone. Schedules use this time zone.
  2. Locks → Register a lock, or run lockguard:sync-locks --register at the server. LockGuard asks you to confirm your password.
  3. Open the lock and switch Commands on when you're happy. Until then LockGuard refuses every command for that lock.

How to: add an individual permission override

  1. Users → click the person → Roles and permissions tab.
  2. Add an override: pick the permission (e.g. Request remote unlocking), choose Allow (or Deny to block something their role normally gives), and add a reason.
  3. Check the preview, which highlights sensitive changes, then save and confirm your password.

How to: check the audit log hasn't been tampered with

php artisan lockguard:audit-verify Turn on two-step sign-in for yourself first thing: open the Account page (on a phone More → Account; on a computer go to /m/account) and choose Two-step verification. A Super Administrator account without it is a very attractive target. With great power comes great responsibility, and about 47 notification emails a day.

Scenario
P

Priya and the 2 a.m. buzz

Security Administrator

At 2:04 a.m. Priya's phone buzzes: Repeated failed sign-ins on Jordan's account, then New device sign-in. Jordan is a resident who's asleep. Priya opens the Security center, suspends Jordan's account (new requests stop instantly), uses Sign out everywhere, and opens an incident. In the morning Jordan explains their old tablet was stolen. How to: handle an alert

  1. Security center → open the alert. Read what triggered it and who's involved.
  2. Acknowledge it so everyone knows you're on it. Some alerts (offline lock, low battery) resolve themselves when the problem clears; you just acknowledge those.
  3. Act: suspend the account, sign them out everywhere, revoke a device, or lock a door.
  4. Resolve the alert, or open an incident if it needs investigating.

How to: run an incident from start to finish

New: just opened. Acknowledged: someone owns it. Investigating: finding out what happened. Contained: the danger is stopped (account suspended, phone revoked). Resolved: fixed. Closed: filed away for good; a closed incident can't be changed, so open a new one if it flares up again.

A fire drill for computer trouble: notice it, stop it spreading, fix it, write down what happened. "Sign out everywhere" ends every session the person has, on every device, but leaves their account active. It's perfect for "I think someone saw my password", combined with a password change. Security Administrator's favourite band? The Locksmiths. Their best album: "Revoked and Reloaded."

Scenario
M

Marcus and the Friday shuffle

Property Manager

Friday at Maple Court: flat 4 moves out, flat 7 moves in, and a plumber is booked for Saturday 9–12. Marcus suspends the old tenant of flat 4 (they can't open anything from that second on), sends an invitation to the new tenant of flat 7, and gives Pat the plumber access to the Back Door from Saturday 09:00 until 12:00. How to: invite a new person

  1. Access management → Invitations → Invite someone.
  2. Type their email (and name, if you like). Add a short personal note; it appears in the email.
  3. Send. LockGuard asks you to confirm your password, then shows the held registration code once. You won't normally need it.
  4. Watch progress in the list: Waiting to accept → training (e.g. "3 of 7 lessons done") → Completed.

How to: give someone access to a door (with a time limit)

  1. Users → click the person → Lock access tab.
  2. Choose the lock. Optionally set From and Until. Leave From empty to start now, and Until empty for no end.
  3. Give access. It shows up under "Locks this person can use".

How to: handle a move-out

  1. Suspend the account (Users → person → Suspend). New requests are blocked immediately.
  2. Remove their lock access on the Lock access tab.
  3. Ask Security to revoke their phone if it might be misused, or a Super Administrator to Revoke the account for good.
Suspending is a pause button and can be undone with Reactivate. Revoking is permanent. When in doubt, suspend. A property manager walks into a bar. Then gives the bar access only until 11 p.m.

Scenario
R

Riley and the grocery bags

Resident

Riley arrives home with six bags of shopping and no free hands. Elbow-tap on the LockGuard icon, Unlock Front Door, confirm. The card shows Unlocking… and then Unlocked once the lock reports in. Later, on the sofa, Riley wonders whether they locked up. One look at Home: Locked, reported by the lock 2 minutes ago. Riley stays on the sofa.

How to: lock a door

  1. Home → find the door's card.
  2. Tap "Lock Front Door". It shows Locking…; the old state stays until the lock reports.
  3. Wait for Confirmed and the big green Locked.

How to: unlock a door

  1. Tap "Unlock Front Door".
  2. Read the warning and tap Unlock. Anyone at the door can open it until it's locked again.
  3. Confirm it's you if asked (password or code). This lasts about 10 minutes.

How to: see who came and went

Tap Activity on the bottom bar. Each line shows the door, the action, who did it, and the result.

LockGuard ties your access to your phone. Lending it to someone is lending them your key, and everything they do is recorded under your name. Why did the smartphone go to therapy? It had too many unresolved locks.

Gale visits for the weekend

How to: use your guest access

  2. Open LockGuard to see the door(s) you were given and when your access ends.
  3. Lock the door whenever you leave, and check it says Locked.
If you see "Your access to this lock has not started yet" or "has ended", that's the time window working. Contact whoever invited you to change it.
Guest access is like a library book: lovely while you have it, and it goes back on time whether you remember or not.

Avery's quarterly check

How to: review the audit log

  1. Security → Audit log.
  2. Filter by category, outcome (success, denied, failed…), lock, and a From/To date range.
  3. Open an event to see every detail: who, what, when, from which device and network address.
  4. Export what you need for your report. The export itself is recorded in the audit log too.
